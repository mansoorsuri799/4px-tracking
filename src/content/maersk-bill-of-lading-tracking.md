# Maersk Bill of Lading Tracking: Track by B/L or Booking

Not every team has the container number on day one. **Maersk bill of lading tracking** (and booking-reference tracking) lets you follow the contractual shipment — including one or more containers — using the IDs on your shipping documents.

## Bill of Lading vs Booking Reference vs Container Number

| ID | Role | When it shines |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Booking reference | Commercial booking created first | Before full documentation is issued |
| Bill of lading (B/L) | Contract of carriage / cargo receipt | After docs are cut; covers the shipment set |
| Container number | One physical unit | Day-to-day location of a specific box |

Use the **booking** while the shipment is still being confirmed. Switch to the **B/L** once documents are available. Drill into each **container** when inland delivery or equipment questions arise.

## How to Track with a Maersk B/L Number

1. Copy the bill of lading number exactly from the PDF or portal — include prefixes and suffixes.  
2. Paste it into the tracker on [maersktracking.com](/).  
3. Note every container listed in the result or on the document annex.  
4. Confirm the same B/L on [maersk.com/tracking](https://www.maersk.com/tracking/) under Ocean (or the correct mode).  
5. Track individual ISO numbers when you need box-level detail — see [Maersk container tracking](/maersk-container-tracking).

## How to Track with a Booking Reference

Booking IDs are ideal early in the lifecycle:

- Space is requested but not all containers are gated in  
- The B/L is not issued yet  
- You only received a booking confirmation email  

If the booking lookup fails, wait for confirmation that the booking is active, or ask whether it was amended into a new reference. Amended bookings are a frequent cause of “not found” results.

## What B/L Tracking Usually Shows

Expect shipment-level milestones such as:

- Booking confirmation and planned vessel / voyage  
- Origin terminal activity for associated containers  
- Departure and arrival of the ocean leg  
- Destination discharge readiness  
- Links or lists of related container numbers  

It may **not** show every inland truck GPS ping. For last-mile style detail on parcels, use the parcel number instead of the ocean B/L.

## Multi-Container Bookings

One B/L can cover several boxes stuffed at different times or yards. Practical workflow:

1. Open B/L tracking for the parent shipment.  
2. Export or write down each container number.  
3. Track each container until gate-out.  
4. Flag any box that lags far behind siblings after discharge.

This prevents false alarms when three containers are delivered and a fourth is still on rail.

## Documents You Should Keep Handy

- Draft and final bill of lading  
- Booking confirmation  
- Shipping instructions / packing list  
- Arrival notice and invoice references (for ops, not usually for trackers)  

When you contact support, the B/L plus booking pair resolves most identity issues faster than an invoice alone.

## Troubleshooting B/L Lookups

- **Wrong mode selected** — Ocean vs Air vs LCL vs Parcel  
- **Draft vs final B/L** — numbers can differ after amendment  
- **House vs master B/L** — NVOCC house bills may need the forwarder’s tool  
- **Typos in prefixes** — copy from the PDF text layer when possible  

If events stall after a valid B/L result, read [Maersk tracking not updating](/maersk-tracking-not-updating). For the full process from first search to timeline reading, use [how to track a Maersk shipment](/how-to-track-maersk-shipment).

## Conclusion

**Maersk bill of lading tracking** is the bridge between commercial paperwork and physical containers. Start with the B/L or booking on [MaerskTracking.com](/), confirm on Maersk’s official tracker, then follow each container number to the gate. That sequence keeps shippers, consignees, and warehouse teams aligned on the same voyage.
