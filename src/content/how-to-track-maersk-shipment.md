# How to Track a Maersk Shipment: Step-by-Step Guide

Tracking a Maersk shipment should feel straightforward — paste the right reference, read the milestones, and know what happens next. This guide walks through **how to track a Maersk shipment** with a container number, bill of lading, or booking reference, whether you use [MaerskTracking.com](/) or Maersk’s official tools.

## What You Need Before You Track

Maersk visibility depends on a valid reference tied to a live booking. Gather one of these from your confirmation email, bill of lading, or forwarder portal:

- **Container number** — ISO format (four letters + seven digits, often with a check digit)  
- **Bill of lading (B/L) number** — contractual shipment ID  
- **Booking reference** — early-stage commercial booking ID  
- **Parcel tracking number** — for Maersk parcel products  

If you only have an invoice or purchase order, ask your shipper or forwarder for the logistics reference. Those commercial documents rarely work in carrier trackers.

## Step 1: Confirm the Booking Type

Maersk’s network covers more than ocean containers. Knowing the mode helps you interpret results:

| Booking type | Typical reference | What the timeline emphasizes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ocean | Container / B/L / booking | Terminal, vessel, and port events |
| Air | Air waybill / booking | Flight and hub handoffs |
| LCL | B/L / booking | Consolidated cargo milestones |
| Parcel | Parcel tracking number | Last-mile style scans |

If you are unsure, start with the number printed largest on your shipping documents — then refine if the first lookup fails.

## Step 2: Track on MaerskTracking.com

1. Open [maersktracking.com](/).  
2. Paste your container, B/L, booking, or parcel number into the tracker.  
3. Optionally select a carrier if the picker helps disambiguate similar formats.  
4. Review the status timeline, latest summary, and any origin/destination details shown.

This independent tracker is useful when you want a fast multi-carrier view alongside partner events. It is **not** a Maersk account login and cannot change bookings, pay invoices, or release holds.

## Step 3: Track on Maersk’s Official Page

For carrier-native detail:

1. Go to [maersk.com/tracking](https://www.maersk.com/tracking/).  
2. Choose Ocean, Air, LCL, or Parcel.  
3. Enter the matching reference.  
4. Open vessel, terminal, and document-related milestones as needed.

Official tracking is the source of truth for Maersk-operated events. Use it when you need confirmation for customs, demurrage discussions, or customer updates.

## Step 4: Read the Timeline Correctly

A healthy ocean timeline usually progresses from gate-in → loaded → departed → (optional transshipment) → arrived → discharged → gate-out. Sparse updates while the vessel is at sea do **not** automatically mean the cargo is stuck.

Watch for:

- **Planned vs actual events** — ETAs move when schedules change  
- **Multiple containers** — one B/L can show several ISO numbers  
- **Inland gaps** — rail or truck legs may update less often than terminals  

For container-specific tips, see [Maersk container tracking](/maersk-container-tracking). For B/L and booking IDs, see [bill of lading tracking](/maersk-bill-of-lading-tracking).

## Step 5: When Results Do Not Appear

If the tracker returns nothing:

1. Re-copy the number — avoid OCR errors from PDFs.  
2. Confirm you are using the right ID type for the booking.  
3. Wait for the first gate-in or booking activation if the cargo just left the shipper.  
4. Ask your forwarder whether the booking was amended or split.  
5. Follow the fixes in [Maersk tracking not updating](/maersk-tracking-not-updating).

## Best Practices for Shippers and Consignees

- Save booking and B/L PDFs in one folder for every voyage  
- Track all containers on the B/L, not only the first listed  
- Share the same reference with warehouse and customs teams  
- Treat ETAs as estimates until discharge and gate-out confirm arrival  
- Contact Maersk or your forwarder through official channels for holds, claims, and documentation  

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Correct reference type selected  
- [ ] Number pasted without spaces or extra characters  
- [ ] Booking type known (Ocean / Air / LCL / Parcel)  
- [ ] All containers on the B/L checked  
- [ ] ETA compared against latest vessel schedule  

## Conclusion

Once you know which ID to use, **Maersk shipment tracking** becomes a repeatable habit: paste the reference, read milestones in order, and escalate only when silence lasts longer than a normal ocean or inland gap. Start with the tracker on [MaerskTracking.com](/), then confirm critical events on Maersk’s official tools when decisions depend on the exact status.
